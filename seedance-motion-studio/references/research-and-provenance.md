# Research, Claims and Provenance

## Purpose
Keep factual material traceable from source to script, storyboard, generated shot and final edit. Visual plausibility is not evidence.

## Claim classes
- `VERIFIED_FACT`: directly supported by a reliable source or primary material.
- `ATTRIBUTED_CLAIM`: someone said or alleged it; the attribution itself is supported, but the claim may not be independently established.
- `ANALYSIS`: reasoned interpretation based on cited evidence.
- `ASSUMPTION`: working premise not yet verified.
- `UNKNOWN`: information that is missing, disputed or not sufficiently supported.

Do not silently promote an attributed claim, analysis or assumption into a verified fact.

## Claim register
For every material claim, record:
- claim ID and exact proposed wording;
- class/status;
- source ID and URL/file;
- publisher/author;
- publication date and access date where relevant;
- supporting passage/data;
- caveats, conflicting sources and confidence;
- where it appears in the video (script beat, shot ID, caption or graphic);
- whether it has been rechecked before delivery.

## Visual provenance
Every documentary-looking visual should be tagged internally as one of:
- `AUTHENTIC_SOURCE_MEDIA`: real photo/video from a traceable source;
- `USER_PROVIDED_MEDIA`: provided by the user, with provenance noted where known;
- `GENERATED_ILLUSTRATION`: AI-generated explanatory or atmospheric visual;
- `RECONSTRUCTION`: a staged or generated visualization of a past/possible event;
- `GRAPHIC_OR_DIAGRAM`: designed explanatory asset;
- `UNKNOWN_PROVENANCE`: provenance unresolved; do not imply it is authentic.

When viewers could mistake generated imagery for real evidence, clearly label it as illustrative in the edit, captions, narration, or context. Never fabricate archival footage, quotes, official documents, logos, charts or evidence and present them as genuine.

## Research discipline
- Prefer primary sources for product features, official announcements, public records, data and technical specifications.
- Use multiple independent reliable sources for disputed or high-impact claims when feasible.
- Check dates and context; a true statement from an old source may no longer be current.
- Record source conflicts rather than forcing false certainty.
- Do not invent a citation or claim that a source was checked if it was not.
- For product videos, verify that depicted capabilities actually exist.
- For science videos, verify causal mechanisms and identify simplifications or metaphors.
- For news videos, distinguish confirmed facts, allegations, analysis and unknowns.

## Before delivery
Check that:
1. every material factual claim has a source or is explicitly qualified;
2. on-screen numbers and labels match the source;
3. the visual's implied status (real evidence vs illustration) is clear;
4. no prompt has introduced invented product behavior or historical detail;
5. source notes are preserved in the project package.
