# Module: News Explainer

## Objective
Help viewers understand what happened, what evidence supports it, why it matters, and what remains unknown. Accuracy and provenance outrank dramatic spectacle.

## Required inputs
- topic and publication time window;
- trusted sources and primary documents where available;
- facts, dates, names, locations, and numbers;
- distinctions between confirmed fact, analysis, allegation, and uncertainty;
- authentic media assets and rights/provenance where available.

## Workflow
1. Build a source-backed timeline.
2. Create a claims register. Each material claim needs a source, date, confidence/status, and wording constraints.
3. Separate actual documentary footage from AI-generated illustrative reconstruction.
4. Choose visual treatment: authentic source footage, maps, timeline, editorial collage, data visualization, or explicitly labeled illustrative scene.
5. Avoid generating fake “evidence” such as fabricated news footage, quotes, logos, or documents.
6. Keep each scene tied to a specific fact, question, or explanatory purpose.
7. Before delivery, check every factual claim and every visual's implied truth status.

## News-specific hard rules
- Never invent dates, quotes, numbers, identities, locations, or event sequences.
- Never present generated reconstructions as authentic footage.
- Label illustrative reconstructions clearly in the edit or surrounding presentation when a viewer could reasonably mistake them for real evidence.
- Attribute claims accurately and distinguish allegations from established findings.
- If sources conflict, report the conflict and avoid false certainty.

## Prompt guidance
For reconstruction shots, describe them as illustrative visualization, not archival evidence. Do not ask Seedance to reproduce precise newspaper text, legal documents, official logos, or data labels; use verified assets and post-production.
