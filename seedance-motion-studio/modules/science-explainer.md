# Module: Science Explainer

## Objective
Make a scientific or technical mechanism understandable without changing the underlying facts. Visual metaphors are useful only when their limits are understood.

## Required inputs
- learning objective and audience knowledge level;
- authoritative sources;
- key concepts, mechanisms, variables, and causal links;
- misconceptions to avoid;
- evidence, diagrams, data, or experiments where available.

## Workflow
1. Write the core explanation in plain language.
2. Map the causal chain and define what changes, what causes it, and what can be observed.
3. Mark which visuals are physically accurate, schematic, metaphorical, or not to scale.
4. Break the mechanism into steps with one learning objective per beat.
5. Select motion language: mechanism build, system flow, data reveal, whiteboard build, generative field, or character analogy.
6. Pilot any visually complex or scientifically sensitive shot.
7. Ask a domain-appropriate reviewer or verify against sources for high-stakes scientific claims.

## Science-specific QA
- Are causal arrows and directions correct?
- Are scales, labels, counts, and proportions truthful where they matter?
- Does the visual metaphor imply a false mechanism?
- Are simplifications disclosed where necessary?
- Are uncertainty and consensus represented fairly?
- Does the viewer learn the intended concept, rather than merely seeing attractive motion?

## Prompt guidance
Describe the mechanism in observable steps. For abstract concepts, state whether the visualization is schematic. Use exact labels and diagrams in post-production when legibility or correctness matters.
